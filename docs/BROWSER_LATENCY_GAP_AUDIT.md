## 0. Ten-line summary

1. The open cells are not one gap. They are three pipelines with three different gaps: kernel-browser **navigation** (+530..+956 ms P95 in all 8 cells), kernel-browser **steady state** (at parity at DPR1; behind on animated content, at DPR2 and on video fps), and the **Room desktop / headed slice** path (key P95 382-432 ms, click up to 1682 ms, 0.2-17 fps, never decomposed).
2. Navigation is lost in **lifecycle**, not per-frame work: Chrome itself is equal on both arms (~190 ms). Chariox then spends ~360 ms median in its own pipeline (new display per document, admission, IDR reset, a lossy 25 KB first frame at QP 51 that cannot meet the 2.5 % settled criterion, no refinement frames while the page is static, a 120-150 ms quiet wait, then a 100-130 KB lossless repair) where Selkies spends ~130 ms sending the next frames of one continuous stream. Measured on b3 with the product's encoder (section 7a): the IDR budget is clamped to 1.5 frames of bitrate on every call; a 6-frame budget converges in 5 frames (84 ms) instead of never; and the navlat Selkies arm had a geometry advantage (1280x800 source) that P0 removes.
3. Bytes per typed-URL navigation are 0.7-1.25 MB vs 0.3 MB: the same pixels are encoded twice (browser_window kind 5 = 295 KB plus visible_page kind 4 = 177 KB), plus auxiliary 107 KB, the lossless repair, and the relay's JSON re-serialization of every binary frame over 16 KiB (+33 %).
4. Steady state is at or ahead of Selkies at DPR1 (keyboard 123 vs 136 ms; mirror scroll 27 vs 155 ms) because the push/ACK gate, native XShm capture and WebCodecs sink were copied from Selkies already. The remaining misses are specific: animated content (+12 ms p50: the single-thread OpenH264 with a per-frame activity scan), DPR2 (+50-80 ms: Chromium repaints at device scale 2 while Selkies gives Retina viewers a DPR1 source), video fps (45 vs 53: viewer presentation), and CPU (0.95 vs 0.72 cores: OpenH264 single-thread 13.6 vs x264 7.5 ms/frame, plus Node and copies).
5. The Room desktop path (WT-22, ND-06, CU-PARITY, takeover) is a different code path (peer capture RPCs with capture credits, a 33 ms event coalescer, stream stalls of 0.5-3 s at scroll start, 66/96 key timeouts at DPR2) that never got the push-pump/ACK-gate treatment; its mirror coverage is 0/12 **by construction** (a Room Environment requires a headed slice, and a headed slice is desktop video).
6. Why 31 phases and 43 rounds stalled: vidlat tuned the steady-state keyboard cell to parity and then stopped at "codec floor" and "viewer, not server"; the structural items of the 10-08 review (one persistent stream per Tab, exact frame within 150 ms of motion stop, one fence per gesture) were never started; navlat r3 concluded "a persistent per-document capture is not justified" against its own stage table; four different stop criteria, three source geometries and two Selkies configurations made rounds incomparable; most rounds ran on b3 under builds; rounds had 120-150 min hard stops, too short for lifecycle work.
7. Selkies' mechanisms that we have not copied and can: one continuous stream per surface with no per-document lifecycle; refinement by continuous P-frames after a change (streaming mode) and a CRF-18 paint-over burst instead of waiting for a lossless repair; x264-style sliced threads (allowed inside OpenH264 as SM_FIXEDSLCNUM with threads > 1); no second encode of the same pixels; a viewer-scaled DPR1 motion source for Retina viewers (for motion only; settled pixels stay exact per the owner's rule).
8. Ranked root causes (section 3): RC1 per-document display lifecycle (8 nav cells + UX-05 + P1 CPU, ~250-300 ms each); RC2 lossy first frame + quiet wait + lossless repair on the critical path (same cells, ~150-200 ms); RC3 double encode + relay JSON envelope + auxiliary streams (bytes 2-4x, CPU 2x per nav); RC4 desktop path serial capture RPCs (6+ cells, 100s of ms, fps 0-17); RC5 DPR2 device-scale raster (DPR2 click/scroll cells, +50-80 ms); RC6 OpenH264 one thread + RC_OFF activity model (CPU +0.2 cores, animated p50 +12 ms); RC7 viewer presentation (video fps -8); RC8 mirror credit batching (V-UX-02, +1 RTT per extra packet); RC9 web-client cold raster (AT-17, 1-12 ms over rule B).
9. Plan order (PLAN.md): P1 persistent per-Tab display + P2 first-frame refinement close the most cells per unit of work and are independent of the multi-domain redesign; P4 (desktop path) is independent only in its push-pump form; the Room-browser mirror coverage (WT-22 mirror dimension) is BLOCKED-BY-REDESIGN.
10. Exit: every cell in section 6 at its threshold in one paired run per host (software x264/OpenH264 on the builder-class host and GPU on Miguel's laptop), diagnostics off, RTT printed next to every P95.

## 3. Ranked root causes (cells explained x milliseconds), with the measurement that proves each

**RC1 — a new display per document (lifecycle on the critical path).** Cells: STAGE-BROWSER-LATENCY-URL x4, -LINK x4, DISPLAY-P1 (CPU/nav), DISPLAY-P1-GATE misses, BROWSER-UX-05, half of DISPLAY-P2. Evidence: navlat r3 link stage table: stream init 178 -> ready 241 -> first page send 365 after the click while Chrome's source stop is 191; navlat r1 PUSH_READY.md:44 "new display per document: admission 70-100 ms, then 170-190 ms to the first frame (IDR reset), then a quality ramp"; UX-05 attribution (browserux LANE_STATUS.md.r36bak:29,49): native attestation (a CDP screenshot of the still-loading page, 190-420 ms) sits behind the viewer's subscribe 310-830 ms after the click. Code: document fencing in K/runtime/state/kernel_browser_runtime.rs:124-150 (`document_id` required), D/kernel-browser-navigation-display.mjs (pre-start + adopt), D/kernel-browser-page-display.mjs subscribeVisiblePage (admission, protection, document fencing), D/kernel-browser-native.mjs:116-123 per-display attestation. Size: ~250-300 ms per navigation (init->ready 65 + admission 70-100 + IDR reset 170-190, partly overlapped). Selkies: none of it exists.

**RC2 — the first frame cannot meet the settled criterion, and nothing refines it until the lossless repair.** Cells: the same 10 + DISPLAY-P1-GATE strict-stop misses + tails of click/type at DPR2. Evidence: navlat r1 PUSH_READY.md:44 "first frame 12.9 % off vs the 2.5 % criterion, ~21 KB IDR budget"; r3 interval medians first->exact send 91 ms, exact send->arrival 68 ms, arrival->paint 36 ms; browserperf r14 misses where the strict stop was never met within 5 s. Code: DN/openh264.c:33-66 (every frame, including the IDR of a new document, is cut to a VBV of 1.5 frames = 25 KB at 8 Mbit/s and 60 fps: `h->fill=fmin(1.5*frame_bits(h),h->fill+frame_bits(h))` runs on every call, so the IDR lands at QP 51 for a Wikipedia page at both geometries (measured, section 7a); inter QP falls one step per frame, which is 16-18 frames to the settled picture, but with a static page the worker reads back nothing (DN/worker.rs:400-432 readback only when dirty) so no refinement frame is ever encoded); DN/codec.c:254-311 same VBV for x264; exact repair only after QUIET 120 ms / quietNativeMs 150 (DN/direct.rs:17-21, D/kernel-browser-display.mjs:214). Selkies: same 1.5-frame VBV in CBR (P/pipeline.rs vbv_tests:828) but streaming mode keeps encoding at 60 fps so the picture converges within a few frames, plus a CRF-18 paint-over burst; in CRF mode (slice default) no VBV at all. Size: ~150-200 ms per navigation plus 97-130 KB.

**RC3 — bytes and CPU: the same pixels encoded twice, auxiliary streams, and the relay JSON envelope.** Cells: DISPLAY-P1 (CPU/nav 1.5-1.8x), URL/LINK tails on the 8 Mbit/s path, MIRROR-P2 idle bytes, every hosted cell on a real uplink. Evidence: navlat r3 byte classes per URL navigation: browser_window (kind 5) 294,939 B and visible_page (kind 4) 176,840 B are both streamed. The window layer is demanded "while a Tab changes, after input that may navigate, and under a non-mirror page" until the new page's preview or mirror painted (W/kernel-browser/band-navigation.ts:86-95 `windowLayerDemand`: `wanted = !previewing && (changing || band.waking && !band.landed || link.waking && !link.landed || paintMode !== "mirror" && !immediate)`), so every typed URL and link click opens a second 1920x1080 / 2560x1600 encode of the same window for the whole navigation, and a page in video fallback streams the window layer alongside its page display for as long as it stays video (W/kernel-browser/display.ts:10 `browser_window` target), browser_auxiliary 107,337 B, relay TLS 661,157 B; relay re-serialization +148 KB per navigation (`PIPELINE_FINDINGS.md:28`; R/server/connection/writer.rs:165-177 on main). ### 3.1 Room desktop path (RC4) — what the code does on main

Full map with file:line: `history/room_desktop_path_codemap.md`. The Room Browser sub-view (W/terminal-os/room-browser.tsx) and the Computer view (W/react/terminal/ViewRouteMount.tsx:365-368) render the same `RoomEnvironmentCanvasSurface`; current web clients (protocol >= 525) send a `sharedDisplay` offer, so main always takes the native CXD1 path (K/runtime/slice_command_executor/display_endpoint.rs:29-44). Selkies is still started inside the slice (D/slice-screen.sh:17) but no current client uses it: the Selkies-in-slice bridge (K/runtime/state/room_display.rs, D/slice-selkies-stream.py) is legacy for older clients.

The pixels come from the same native display worker as the kernel browser, but running inside the slice container (D/kernel-desktop-source.mjs:19-21,111-135 spawns `chariox-kernel --display-native-worker`; protection oracle masks every readback). The difference is the loop around it:

1. **One peer RPC per frame, serial, over the relay.** The home kernel's push pump issues each credit as a `RoomSharedDisplay{Capture{after_sequence,reset,congested}}` peer RPC home -> relay -> worker kernel -> Node controller -> native worker and waits for the reply before the next credit (K/transport/relay_client/display_pump.rs:512-531; K/runtime/state/room_shared_display.rs:482-532, 15 s timeout). The direct-frame shortcut is never used (room capture returns `lease=None`, display_pump.rs:373-410), and the path always goes through the relay even when the slice is local (K/runtime/state/room_browser_controller.rs:67-95). Effective fps = 1 / (home<->relay<->worker RTT + worker wait), on top of the ACK gate. With the relay on b3 and the worker on a rented host that is 60-90 ms per frame: the 11-17 fps slicemem measured, and 0-5 fps once the gate closes. Selkies: the capture thread hands frames to the socket with no request at all.
2. **One round trip and one process per input event.** The web client's `RoomScreenInputQueue` is strictly serial (cloud packages/terminal-domain/src/room/screen-input-queue.ts:4-14); the home kernel admits each action by polling every 10 ms (K/runtime/state/human_environment_action_runtime_state.rs:24,319) and sends a `ComputerInput` peer RPC; the worker spawns a new `/opt/chariox-slice/slice-screen.sh` process per event (K/runtime/state/tool_dispatch/slice.rs:842-870,1044-1300), which runs `xdotool` for humans (10 s timeout) or `slice-keyboard.py` XTest for agents, 40 ms per key (D/slice-screen.sh:601-699). A key therefore costs viewer -> home -> relay -> worker -> fork/exec -> X -> frame RPC -> relay -> viewer, serialised with the previous key. This is the 198-233 ms p50 and the 66/96 timeouts at DPR2 in slicemem r5. Selkies: `xtest.fake_input` + `flush()` inline, no round trip.
3. **DPR2 adds nothing and removes nothing**: the desktop stays 1280x800 physical and dsf = 2 halves the CSS size (D/kernel-desktop-display.mjs:36-43). The DPR2 cells fail harder only because the viewer is slower to decode/present at 2x canvas size while the serial loop is the same.
4. **No mirror by construction**: `mirror_*` ops exist only in the per-user `KernelBrowserHost` on the host kernel (D/kernel-browser-host.mjs:73,826,892-894); the slice controller serves `room.display` and `computer.*` only (D/browser-controller.mjs:58-63); the web Room surface always sets `paintMode=video` (W/kernel/browser-room-shared-display.ts:51). The slice Chromium is Debian apt chromium (D/Dockerfile:102-113), not the pinned Chrome for Testing, although it does expose CDP on 127.0.0.1:9222 (D/slice-screen.sh:270-279).

So RC4 is two loops to replace, not a tuning problem: the worker must push frames (same display_pump semantics, running where the worker is, with the home kernel relaying sealed frames) and input must be injected without a per-event process or a per-event round trip. The 33 ms coalescer culinux found is the local kernel websocket write delay (K/config.rs:69, `CHARIOX_KERNEL_WRITE_DELAY_MS`), not a relay timer; TCP_NODELAY is set on main.

## 4. Why the earlier rounds could not move the open cells

1. **They optimised a cell that is now closed and left the open ones untouched.** vidlat r1-r46 drove keyboard-to-pixel on a static page to parity (123 vs 136 ms) by copying Selkies' ACK gate, decoder worker and sink. Navigation (the +500-950 ms cells) and the desktop path were never in its workload. The 10-08 review named the lifecycle items (one persistent stream, exact frame within 150 ms of motion stop, one fence per gesture) as Phase 2; displayopus FINAL-r3.md:51-56 records Phase 2.1 "never started", Phase 3 handed off.
2. **The last navigation lane looked at its own stage table and decided against the structural fix.** navlat r3 PUSH_READY.md:107 "a persistent per-document capture is judged not justified" although its medians show stream init at 178 ms and first page send at 365 ms after a click whose source finished at 191 ms, and startup plus repair at 156 ms of the 362 ms remaining pipeline. It then spent the round on the hidden-omnibox frames, which it showed were outside the measured windows.
3. **Accepted floors.** "Cores = codec floor (OpenH264)" (vidlat r45, accepted 10-19) and "DPR1 fps loss is the viewer, not the server" closed the two CPU/fps gaps without a fix path. OpenH264 was never threaded like x264 (one thread below 2.5 MP) and the viewer sink was only partly moved off canvas.
4. **Wrong layer for the fix in several rounds.** Encoder knobs, pump-gate tuning, relay window sizes (r45-46: transfers 1235 -> 155 ms, keyboard and fps unchanged), band inventory polling (r28: cores down, fps unmoved) were each real but off the critical path of the open cells.
5. **Measurement artefacts and incomparable rounds.** The Selkies arm of the navigation cells streamed a fixed 1280x800 source (viewer-upscaled) while Chariox streamed 1920x1080 or 2560x1600: at the same 8 Mbit/s and 1.5-frame VBV a 25 KB IDR is near-converged at 1280x800 and at QP 51 at our geometries (section 7a), so part of the navigation gap in navlat r3 is geometry, not pipeline (P0 fixes it; P2 is still required because our stream emits no refinement frames at all). Four stop criteria (DOM + two RAF, glyph detection, exact native text, physical headline region), three source geometries (1280x800 at viewer DPR, 2560x1600, 1920x1080), two Selkies configurations (slice: 30 fps CRF 25; A/B: 60 fps CBR 8000), Chrome stable vs CfT on some arms (+30-50 ms on the omnibox list paint), typing at 5 ms/char on the native arm only (r1), and most rounds on b3 under cargo builds (fsync p99 100-300 ms). A number from one round rarely meant the same thing in the next.
6. **Architecture limit.** Four processes and per-frame checks (document fencing, protection, attestation) mean every per-stage gain is bounded by the handoffs around it; the 10-08 review called this "wrong loop shape, not slow stages". Only the direct lease (L3) attacked it, and only for whole frames during motion.
7. **Round shape.** 120-150 min hard stops with a publish per round favour knob changes with a same-round number over a two-day lifecycle change.
